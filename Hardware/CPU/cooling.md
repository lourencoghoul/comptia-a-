## CPU Cooling

Cooling is the system responsible for controlling the temperature of computer components, especially the CPU. Without proper cooling, the processor can overheat, lose performance, or even be damaged.

---

## Why It Is Important

The CPU generates heat continuously. If the temperature gets too high:
- The system may reduce performance automatically (**thermal throttling**)  
- Unexpected shutdowns can occur for protection  
- There is a risk of long-term hardware damage  

---

## Main Types of Cooling

### 1. Air Cooling
- Uses a heatsink and fan  
- Most common and affordable  
- Easy to install and maintain  

**Recommended for:**
- Everyday use  
- Study environments  
- Mid-range systems  

---

### 2. Liquid Cooling
- Uses liquid to dissipate heat  
- Can be:
  - AIO (All-in-One)
  - Custom loop (advanced)

**Advantages:**
- Higher cooling efficiency  
- Often quieter operation  

**Recommended for:**
- High-performance systems  
- Overclocking setups  

---

## Key Components

- **Heatsink:** absorbs heat from the CPU  
- **Fan:** pushes hot air away  
- **Thermal Paste:** improves heat transfer between CPU and cooler  

---

## Relation to Overclocking

When overclocking:
- The CPU generates more heat  
- Efficient cooling becomes essential  

Without proper cooling, the system may become unstable or suffer damage.

---

## Best Practices

- Keep the system clean (avoid dust buildup)  
- Ensure proper airflow inside the case  
- Replace thermal paste when needed  
- Monitor temperatures using software tools  

---

## Summary

- Cooling = temperature control  
- Essential for performance and hardware safety  
- Air cooling = simple and sufficient for most users  
- Liquid cooling = more powerful, used in advanced setups
